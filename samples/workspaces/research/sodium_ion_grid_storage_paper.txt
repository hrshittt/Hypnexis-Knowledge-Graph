Sodium-Ion Batteries for Grid Storage: A Field Evaluation at the Harbor Storage Facility

Priya Natarajan and Ravi Menon
Voltaic Systems; Kestrel University
Published 2023

Abstract

We report eighteen months of field data from 200 megawatt-hours of sodium-ion storage at the Harbor Storage Facility. The batteries retained 96 percent of their capacity and replaced the evening output of the Harbor Coal Plant. We find that sodium-ion chemistry is a viable alternative to lithium-ion for grid storage in coastal climates.

Introduction

Grid operators need storage that avoids cobalt and lithium supply constraints. The problem of seasonal capacity fade in humid coastal sites has not been studied for sodium-ion batteries. This paper presents the first long-run field evaluation of sodium-ion storage on a utility grid.

Data

The dataset contains five-minute telemetry from 1,600 battery modules between January 2022 and June 2023. Weather records come from the Port Calder airport station.

Methods

We use a degradation model that separates calendar ageing from cycle ageing. We compare measured capacity with laboratory cycling tests performed at Kestrel University.

Results

The modules retained 96 percent of rated capacity after eighteen months. Round-trip efficiency averaged 89 percent. Capacity fade was 30 percent lower than the laboratory tests predicted.

Limitations

The evaluation covers a single site and one battery supplier. Eighteen months is short relative to the expected fifteen-year service life.

Future Work

Future work will extend monitoring to the second phase of the Harbor Storage Facility and compare sodium-ion with iron-air storage.
